Erik Jensko

Theoretical cosmology and gravitation

I'm an EPSRC Doctoral Prize Research Fellow in the Department of Mathematics at University College London, working on dark matter, dark energy and modified gravity. My current research focusses on interactions in the dark sector, the thermodynamics of cosmological fluids, and the stability of models that extend the standard cosmological picture.

I study these models from their underlying action through to their cosmological perturbations and observational predictions. In particular, I'm interested in interactions that change the growth of structure while leaving the expansion history unchanged.

I completed my PhD in Mathematics at UCL in 2023, supervised by Prof. Christian Böhmer. Before coming to UCL, I studied theoretical physics at the University of Nottingham.

Recent work

Dark sector interactions

Intrinsic entropy couplings

With Elsa Teixeira and Vivian Poulin, I studied models in which the intrinsic entropy of dark matter couples to scalar field dark energy. These interactions leave the background expansion unchanged, but modify the dark matter Euler equation and can suppress or enhance the growth of structure.

Relativistic fluids

Non-minimal fluid Lagrangian couplings

When a fluid is coupled directly to curvature, the choice of fluid action matters. With Christian Böhmer and Eissa Al-Nasrallah, I examined how the Brown and Schutz formulations lead to different fluid equations and effective thermodynamic quantities in these models.

  • Read the paper

Dark energy and geometry

Geometric k-essence

With Lehel Csillag, I studied how a simple action in a geometry with vectorial nonmetricity can reproduce purely kinetic k-essence. We used dynamical systems methods, stability conditions and late-time cosmological data to identify and constrain physically viable solutions.

Current research

Cosmological fluids and effective field theory

I'm interested in the relation between thermodynamic descriptions of matter and effective field theory. My ongoing work looks at perturbative stability, entropy degrees of freedom, and the consequences of dark sector interactions for linear and nonlinear cosmology.
